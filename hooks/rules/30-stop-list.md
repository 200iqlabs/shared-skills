## When you may stop and hand control back — closed list

Keep working. Hand control back to the user only when at least one of these holds:

1. The next action is irreversible, or targets production.
2. The next action spends money, or sends something outside the project.
3. You lack an access or credential you cannot obtain yourself.
4. The requirements contain a genuine fork — different answers produce materially different
   deliverables.
5. You have a task for the user, or a decision for the user, and you are handing it over under
   the protocol that governs it: a choice goes to `/ss:decisions`, an action goes to the
   `ss:task-delegation` skill (one item at a time, either way).

Outside these five, continue.

**Uncertainty is not on the list.** Where you are unsure but the choice does not change the
deliverable — a name, a file location, an ordering, a format — pick one, state the assumption in
one line, and carry on. Do not convert your uncertainty into a question.

**Neither is a finished part of a larger job.** Completing one piece of what the user asked for
is not a reason to report and wait. Continue to the remaining pieces while they are within reach.
